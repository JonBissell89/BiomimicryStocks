## SCTTF — Scott Technology (NZX SCT NZ$2.80, ~US$1.68 OTC; mkt cap ~NZ$238M/US$141M, 14 Aug 2026)
Dunedin-based smart automation/robotics: meat & poultry processing (BladeStop, Poultry Trusser), materials handling, mining. FY26 guidance NZ$290–296m revenue and NZ$34–36m operating EBITDA — record year; HY26 EBITDA NZ$13.0m (+7%), net margin held at 29%, forward work +8% to NZ$177m. FY25 earnings +83% on flat revenue as it exited low-margin work. Moat: core protein portfolio highly differentiated — automated poultry/lamb/beef systems with little direct competition; installed base at JBS (majority shareholder), Tyson, Cargill; growing lifecycle-service revenue. Cost compression is the product: automation vs. labor scarcity in food plants. Destination 2030 targets NZ$530m revenue. Risks: small scale, project-based delivery, Marel/JBT in adjacent poultry. ## IHS — IHS Holding (NYSE $8.31, mkt cap ~$2.79B, 11 Aug 2026)
Largest independent emerging-market tower company: 37,672 towers, 55,205 tenants (1.47x colocation) across Nigeria and Africa. Q2 2026 revenue from continuing ops $428.6m (+10.4%, mostly naira FX tailwind; organic just +0.5%); IFRS net loss $7.5m; Rwanda portfolio sold Oct 2025. The business is genuine essential infrastructure — shared towers compress the cost of African connectivity. But the decisive fact: MTN Group is acquiring IHS at $8.50/share (~$6.2B EV, ~$2.2B for shares MTN doesn't own); shareholders approved the deal in August 2026 and IHS will delist and become a wholly owned MTN subsidiary. Upside is capped at a ~2% arb spread — it cannot be a Top-25 compounder. ## WRTBY — Wärtsilä (ADR $6.70, mkt cap ~$19.8B, 19 Aug 2026)
Finnish maker of engines and lifecycle services for marine propulsion and flexible power plants (grid balancing, now data-center power). H1 2026: order intake +23% to EUR 4,934m (Q2 +33%, all-time high; Energy Q2 beat prior record by >70% incl. 1.2 GW firm data-center orders and >0.5 GW balancing orders); order book +13% to EUR 8,976m; Q2 operating margin 13.4% vs 11.5%, hitting the 14% target zone. Energy Storage moved to held-for-sale June 2026 (portfolio focus). Moat: huge installed engine base feeding decades of service revenue (~half of sales), multi-fuel (methanol/ammonia-ready) technology leadership vs MAN ES, Rolls-Royce, Caterpillar. System role: balancing engines make high-renewables grids workable; marine efficiency cuts fuel per ton-mile. Risks: long-run shipping electrification, rich valuation (~29x). ## TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor (NYSE ADR $417.69, mkt cap ~$2.17T, 25 Aug 2026)
World's dominant chip foundry (~90% of leading-edge capacity). Q2 2026: revenue $40.2B (+33.7% YoY), gross margin 67.7% (+9.1pts YoY), net margin 55.6%, net income +77%; Q3 guide $44.6–45.8B (+37% YoY midpoint); FY26 revenue growth guided slightly above +40%. Judged honestly on "durable human need enabler": yes — computation is now permanent civilizational substrate (healthcare, grids, communication, water, transport all run on chips), and TSMC is its manufacturing backbone; falling cost per transistor is the purest cost-compression engine in the group. Moat: extreme manufacturing difficulty, process leadership (N2 ramping), ecosystem lock-in; no dilution, fortress balance sheet, fabs expanding in Arizona/Japan/Germany. Key honest caveats: AI-capex cyclicality and Taiwan geopolitical concentration cap survivability. Sources: https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2026/q2 ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001046179/000104617926000451/a2q26e_withguidancexfinal.htm ; https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/tsm/market-cap/

## TOELY — Tokyo Electron (ADR $184.10, mkt cap ~$165B, 15 Aug 2026)
Japan's largest semiconductor production equipment maker: coater/developers (~90% global share, pairs with every ASML litho tool), etch, deposition, cleaning. Q1 FY2027 (Jun-2026 qtr): net sales ¥732.3B (+33.3% YoY, record), operating margin 28.9%; H1 guide raised to ¥1,620B (+37%) and full-year ¥3T+; WFE market now seen growing >20% in both CY2026 and CY2027 with AI data-center demand becoming the dominant driver. Judged honestly: one layer removed from the human need, but it is the toolset that keeps computation's cost falling — a genuine enabler, in a structural oligopoly (AMAT, Lam, KLA, ASML) with deep customer co-development lock-in. Net cash, buybacks, no dilution. Risks: brutal WFE cyclicality; China export-control exposure (~40% of sales historically). ## KRMKF — Kromek Group (AIM KMK ~7.2–8.9p, ~US$0.10 OTC; mkt cap ~£58M/~$78M, Aug 2026)
UK maker of cadmium-zinc-telluride (CZT) radiation detectors: medical imaging components (SPECT; photon-counting CT enablement/licensing with Siemens Healthineers), CBRN/nuclear-security detectors, aviation security. FY26 (to 30 Apr 2026): revenue in line at ~£27.2m, PBT ~£2.15m — first real profitability, but substantially from the Siemens "enablement agreement's exceptional contribution"; £8.8m new orders in H2; planning 8–10x CZT furnace capacity expansion; medium-term target £60m revenue/30% EBITDA. Defense check: CBRN products are protective detection (dirty bombs, contamination), not weapons — no hard rejection on war economics, though government procurement delays already bit in FY26. Decisive negatives: 655m shares after a decade of dilutive placings (+5.3% last year), micro-cap scale, single-partner profit concentration, historically stagnant commercialization. ## GUER — Guerrilla RF (OTCQX ~$3.50, mkt cap ~$40M, Aug 2026)
Fabless RF/microwave semiconductor designer (low-noise and power amplifiers) for 5G infrastructure, SATCOM, automotive telematics, repeaters/DAS and military communications. Q2 2026: record revenue $8.01m (+49.2%), gross margin 70.9%, $1.0m operating income but net loss $(2.23)m; operating cash flow +$2.4m; record $13.6m backlog. Defense check: aerospace & defense was ~27% of Q2 revenue and quadrupled YoY, "driven primarily by strength in the drone and satellite communications markets" — in 2026 the drone RF boom is substantially military; the marginal growth story leans on weapons-platform components, tripping the war-economics hard-rejection screen at the margin. Also: micro-cap with net losses, financing/dilution needs, and giant substitutes (Qorvo, Skyworks, ADI, Mini-Circuits) in catalog RF. Good engineering; wrong profile for a system-improving compounder. ## ECELF — Eurocell plc (~116.5p LSE; ~$1.57 OTC; mkt cap ~£114M/$154M)
UK's leading maker/recycler of PVC-U window, door and roofline systems. FY2025: revenue £403.5m (+13%, but organic flat; volumes -2% on weak RMI market); adjusted operating profit £24.1m (+6%); gross margin slipped to ~50.9% on branch price competition. Pre-IFRS-16 net debt £22.1m after the Alunet aluminium-systems acquisition (£46.7m sales, +28%). Circularity is real: largest UK PVC-U recycling plant, ~3m frames/yr, 30% recycled content in production. Returned £11.4m (6.4p dividend + £5m buyback; ~3.3m shares retired). Five-year target £500m revenue/£50m profit. Strength: closed-loop recycling and vertical integration. Weakness: single-market (UK), housing-cycle dependent, capex-heavy branches, flat organic growth — not a global compounder.
