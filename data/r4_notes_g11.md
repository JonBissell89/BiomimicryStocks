# Round 4 — Group 11 notes (researched August 2026)

## CRMD — CorMedix (~$7.85, ~$620M cap)
DefenCath is the first and only FDA-approved antimicrobial catheter lock solution (taurolidine/heparin) preventing catheter-related bloodstream infections in hemodialysis — a genuine cost-compressor since each CRBSI costs tens of thousands in hospitalization. Q2 2026: consolidated revenue $101.9M (DefenCath $66.1M, Melinta portfolio $35.8M), net income $26.0M, adj EBITDA $58.7M; FY26 guidance $325–345M revenue, $125–140M EBITDA. Supply agreements now cover all top-5 US dialysis organizations. Melinta acquisition (Sep 2025, $300M: $260M cash + $40M stock, funded partly by $150M convertible) diversified into acute-care anti-infectives. Historical dilution was heavy pre-approval but the company is now self-funding. Key risk: post-TDAPA reimbursement cliff (~mid-2027) and inpatient/TPN expansion execution.

## SPRY — ARS Pharmaceuticals (~$5.65, ~$0.5B cap)
neffy, the first needle-free intranasal epinephrine for anaphylaxis, meets a permanent need and improves carry/use compliance, but it is not cheaper per dose than autoinjectors — cost compression is indirect (earlier treatment, fewer ER escalations). Q2 2026: total revenue $33.7M (neffy net product $26.2M), market share 5% (up from 2.5%), 16,000 prescribers; but operating expenses were $95.1M (SG&A $77.6M) and net loss $62.3M. Cash $143.8M with H2-2026 cash opex guided $100–110M despite >40% SG&A cuts; management claims cash-flow breakeven only by end-2027 — dilution risk is real. Competitive substitution risk from Aquestive's oral-film epinephrine and generic autoinjectors. EU (ALK) and Japan partnerships provide licensing-style expansion.

## ADMA — ADMA Biologics (~$9.54, ~$2.25B cap)
Vertically integrated US plasma company: ASCENIV (RSV-hyperimmune IVIG for refractory primary immunodeficiency), BIVIGAM (standard IVIG), Nabi-HB, plus 10 FDA-licensed collection centers and its own fractionation plant. Q2 2026: revenue $124.4M (+2% YoY), gross margin 69% (vs 55%), ASCENIV +24% to $102.9M (H1 $200.4M, +25.5%), GAAP net income $37.8M, adj EBITDA $61.8M; cash $136M, net leverage <0.5x, targeting $200M+ buybacks (reverse dilution after heavy pre-2021 dilution). FY26 guidance was cut from >$635M to $530–560M on aggressive standard-IG pricing/channel inventory (BIVIGAM). Moat: proprietary high-titer plasma donor screening, FDA-licensed manufacturing, yield enhancement. Real-world data (127 patients) shows ASCENIV significantly cuts hospitalizations, antibiotic and steroid use — genuine cost compression, though at premium price. Risks: CSL/Grifols/Takeda scale, standard-IG price war, product concentration.

## KMDA — Kamada (~$8.38, ~$490M cap)
Israeli plasma-derived specialty therapeutics maker with six FDA-approved products: KEDRAB (rabies immune globulin), CYTOGAM (CMV prophylaxis in transplant), VARIZIG, WINRHO SDF, HEPAGAM B, GLASSIA (AATD, royalties from Takeda), plus an Israel/LatAm distribution segment. Q2 2026: record revenue $54.9M (+23% YoY), adj EBITDA $14.1M (26% margin), net income $9.3M (+26%); H1 revenue $100.2M (+13%); FY26 guidance affirmed at $200–205M revenue, $50–53M EBITDA — all organic. TTM net income $22.3M; declared $0.17/share dividend (Aug 17, 2026) — self-funding, no recent dilution (last raise: 2023 FIMI placement). Moat: FDA-licensed fractionation, own US plasma collection, niche hyperimmunes with few rivals (Grifols HyperRAB main KEDRAB competitor). Prophylaxis products (rabies, CMV, hepatitis B) prevent catastrophic costs — real cost compression. Risks: small scale, inhaled-AAT pipeline uncertainty, CFO departure end-2026.

## BORAY — Bora Pharmaceuticals (~$2.39 OTC, ~$1.83B cap; TWSE 6472)
Taiwan-based CDMO plus US specialty-generics group (TWi, Upsher-Smith) plus consumer health (Weider Global Nutrition). Q2 2026 was a record: revenue NT$5.89bn (+47% QoQ), gross margin 41%, EPS NT$4.36, CDMO 12-month backlog at a record US$317M with US$378M newly signed, including a 10+2-year contract with a top-20 pharma at Maple Grove (commercial output Q3 2027) and Maryland injectables online. But TTM tells the strain: revenue $614.7M (+0.3%), net income $48.6M (-62%), EPS -71%, stock -52% over a year; Q1 2026 Upsher-Smith generics revenue ran 18.6% below trend on pricing/demand swings. Growth is serial M&A (Upsher-Smith, Emergent Camden site, MacroGenics CDMO, Weider) funded with debt/equity — integration and leverage risk. CDMO/generics genuinely compress system drug costs, but the pharma side is commodity-like and the US listing is an illiquid pink-sheet line.

## SHWGF — Shandong Weigao (1066.HK HK$3.48, ~HK$15.7B / ~US$2.0B cap)
China's largest medical-consumables group: infusion sets, syringes, needles, blood bags, blood management, orthopedic implants (Weigao Ortho), interventional products, plus US-based Argon Medical. H1 2026: revenue RMB6,849M (+3.1% YoY) but net income RMB774M (-23.2%, EPS RMB0.17 vs 0.22) on rising costs and continued volume-based-procurement (VBP) price cuts across consumables and orthopedics. Share price HK$3.48; 1-year TSR -47%, 5-year -68% — a long value-destruction record despite dividends and no dilution. The company genuinely supplies low-cost essential devices (system cost compression), but as an undifferentiated commodity maker it is the one being compressed: VBP transfers the savings to hospitals while margins erode. Moat is scale and hospital distribution only; substitution among domestic consumable makers is easy. Mature, stagnant top line, shrinking profitability, no visible re-acceleration catalyst.

## TSNLF — Tristel plc (AIM:TSTL ~420p, ~£200M / ~US$270M cap; OTC ~US$5.67)
UK maker of proprietary chlorine dioxide (ClO2) high-level disinfection consumables (wipes/foams) for ultrasound probes, nasendoscopes and ophthalmic devices — a razor-blade recurring model with ~80% gross margins. FY2026 (ended 30 Jun 2026): revenue +10% to £51.1M, adjusted PBT at least £11.5M (+14%); H1 FY26 net income £3.95M (+45%). Net cash, dividend-paying, essentially no dilution. Moat: patented chemistry, hundreds of country registrations, and US regulatory wins — Tristel ULT is the only FDA-cleared ClO2 foam for ultrasound probes, Tristel OPH newly cleared for ophthalmic devices, and AIUM guidelines now accept ClO2 for HLD. Strong cost compression: a 3-minute manual ClO2 process replaces capital-intensive automated reprocessors (e.g., Nanosonics trophon), cutting cost and turnaround per disinfection while preventing device-borne infections. Risks: small scale, US adoption pace against trophon's installed base, CEO transition, single-chemistry concentration.
