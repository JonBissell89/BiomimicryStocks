## SRTA — Strata Critical Medical (~$6.03, ~$540M cap) — 72 — CUT
Former Blade Air Mobility, now pure-play organ-transplant logistics (air/ground) plus clinical services (organ recovery, NRP, perfusion). Q2 2026 revenue $72.5M, +60.7% YoY; ~84% transplant-related; clinical revenue +22.6% sequentially to $24.3M. Three perfusion/recovery bolt-on acquisitions closed in Q2. Balance sheet strong: ~$200M cash, no debt — but still net-loss-making, with profitability targeted in 2026. Serves a permanent, life-critical need and raises organ utilization (average transplant distances +68% since 2018 favor its flight-hour model). Decisive weakness: it is the smaller (#2) services layer against TransMedics ($600M revenue, proprietary OCS hardware plus its own aviation fleet), and pure logistics is substitutable. ## SAEYY — Redcare Pharmacy (~$7.00 ADR, ~$1.5B cap) — 67 — CUT
Europe's largest online pharmacy (Shop Apotheke brands), 14.7M active customers in 7 countries. Q2 2026: revenue €853M +20% YoY (accelerating from 18%), Rx +34%, Germany Rx +58% with 1.7M active Rx customers; adjusted EBITDA margin 3.5% — a decade high; FY guidance raised to 15–17% growth; international segment turning profitable. CardLink e-prescription redemption drove share of German online eRx fulfillment to ~40% of eRx online volume (~67% of the online-Rx market). Genuine cost compression vs. brick-and-mortar and a real chronic-medication service. But it is still a thin-margin retail/logistics layer: net loss ~€38–48M TTM, regulated Rx pricing, DocMorris/Amazon substitution, stock down ~42% in a year. ## WST — West Pharmaceutical Services (~$353.27, ~$25.5B cap) — 84 — ADVANCE
World leader in elastomeric stoppers/plungers and containment-delivery systems for injectable drugs; components are written into customers' regulatory drug filings, making substitution require re-qualification — a deep switching-cost moat (competitors: Datwyler, Aptar, Stevanato). Q2 2026: revenue $872M, +13% organic; adjusted EPS $2.37, +29%; FY26 guidance raised to $3.345–3.38B revenue and 10–11% organic growth. High-value products now 49% of revenue; GLP-1 elastomer components growing high-teens, boosted by Medicare expansion and generic GLP-1 rollout; EU Annex 1 sterility rules force upgrades to West's premium components. Decades of profitability, strong balance sheet, buybacks, negligible dilution. Every injectable dose needs containment — permanent, disruption-resistant need. ## BSX — Boston Scientific (~$49.86, ~$72.3B cap) — 73 — CUT
Diversified medtech leader in minimally invasive cardiovascular devices (Farapulse PFA ablation, Watchman LAA closure, stents, endoscopy). Q2 2026: revenue $5.442B, +7% organic, adj EPS $0.86 — a beat — yet FY26 organic growth guidance slashed again to 5–6% (from double digits in 2025) on a sharp U.S. Watchman slowdown (3% growth, H2 expected to decline mid-to-high single digits) and intensifying PFA competition from J&J/Medtronic; $500M restructuring announced to protect margins. An August cybersecurity incident disrupted global operations, adding uncertainty. Devices reduce trauma/cost vs. open surgery (real cost compression) and the franchise is profitable and durable, but the two franchises that justified a premium are stalling and first-mover PFA advantage is eroding fast. ## RMD — ResMed (~$232.57, ~$33.6B cap) — 79 — ADVANCE
Global leader (~60% share) in CPAP/APAP devices, masks (recurring consumables), and out-of-hospital care software. Q4 FY26 (June qtr): revenue $1.46B, +9%; non-GAAP EPS $2.95, +16%, a beat. FY27 guide: $5.75–5.85B revenue, EPS $12.00–12.25, 5–7% core growth — conservative, with a $75M/-$0.15 headwind from Astral ventilator sales suspension. Net-cash balance sheet, buybacks, decades of profitability. Moat: mask razor-blade model, cloud adherence ecosystem (AirView, billions of nights of data), reimbursement rails, and Philips still hobbled post-recall. GLP-1 disruption fear is fading — ResMed data shows GLP-1 patients are MORE likely to start PAP therapy, and OSA remains ~80% undiagnosed worldwide. Moderate growth but highly durable compounder. ## CLPBY — Coloplast (~$6.70 ADR, ~$15.3B cap) — 78 — ADVANCE
Danish leader in intimate healthcare: ostomy bags, continence catheters (new Luja platform), wound care (Kerecis fish-skin grafts), interventional urology. Q3 FY25/26 (reported Aug 18, 2026): 9M organic growth 6% (Q3 5%); Ostomy +5%, Continence +8%; chronic care drove 72% of growth; double-digit US growth in both chronic franchises; FY guidance maintained at 5–6% organic with ~28% EBIT margin ambitions. Users are fitted once and rarely switch — daily, reimbursed, life-sustaining consumables with nurse-network distribution moat (rivals: Hollister, ConvaTec). Serving unavoidable chronic conditions (stoma, spinal injury) rather than monetizing preventable sickness; better appliances cut leakage/skin complications and total care cost. Slow but exceptionally durable compounder; China softness is the main drag. ## AGLFF — Angelalign Technology (6699.HK ~HK$94.75 / ~US$12.07, ~HK$16.1B / ~$2.1B cap) — 71 — CUT
China's #1 clear-aligner maker, now the global challenger to Align/Invisalign. H1 2026 positive profit alert: case volume +40% YoY to ~316,600; revenue +40%+ to ~$229–231M; net profit +69–79% to ~$24–25.4M. FY2025: revenue $370.3M +37.8%; international revenue doubled to $163M and international case volume (+82%) has now passed domestic. Real cost compression — aligners priced well below Invisalign, fitting China's volume-procurement era and expanding orthodontic access globally. Moats: proprietary materials, a large Asian-dentition biomechanics database, low-cost manufacturing. But net margin is only ~7%, orthodontics is partly discretionary/cosmetic, dentists can switch aligner platforms easily, and a price war plus Align's iTero scanner ecosystem cap pricing power.
