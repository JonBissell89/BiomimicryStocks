# Round 2 judgment rubric (applied identically to every candidate)

Verdict per company, recorded in r2_verdicts CSVs:
- **A** — clearly fits philosophy: product demonstrably serves one of the 7 durable needs
  (food, water, shelter, energy, health, transport/mobility, essential infrastructure) AND
  makes that system more efficient/balanced/circular/resilient/decentralized/self-correcting,
  or lowers real cost per useful outcome. Advances to R3.
- **B** — plausible fit, uncertain mechanism or mixed business. Advances to R3 only if
  needed to fill the 200–300 target, ranked below A.
- **C** — cut. One-line reason required. Typical cuts:
  - keyword-matcher: industry label fits but the actual product doesn't serve the need
  - undifferentiated commodity producer (oil E&P, bulk shipping, generic miner)
  - monetizes the problem rather than fixing it (cost-inflation, scarcity, chronic dependence)
  - hard-rejection economics (weapons, addiction, artificial scarcity, planned obsolescence,
    broken-system maintenance, factory-farm waste concentration, water-scarcity profiteering)
  - single unproven scientific breakthrough required (pre-revenue moonshot biotech/fusion/quantum)
  - pure financial intermediation / rent extraction
  - geographically-trapped slow utility with no scalable product layer (the "local water utility" case)
  - stagnant/contracting commercialization (revenue shrinking, no expansion signal)
  - dilution machine (nano-cap serially issuing shares, price < $0.10 history)

Notes for consistency:
- Regulated utilities: cut UNLESS they have a scalable product/licensing/operating-platform layer
  beyond their captive territory (e.g., water tech subsidiary, multi-country concession model).
- Clinical-stage biotech: cut when value depends on one binary unproven trial; keep commercial-stage
  or platform companies with approved/marketed products serving durable health needs.
- Defense-mix industrials: keep only if civilian share dominates and product serves durable need.
- ADRs/foreign OTC: judged identically to US names.
- Software: keep only software that runs physical-need systems (water, grid, health, logistics,
  agriculture, construction) with evidence of cost compression; cut generic SaaS/adtech/fintech.
- REITs/asset owners: cut unless they operate essential infrastructure with a replicable model
  (towers, water assets) AND show real growth economics.
